Displacement convexity

$$\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^n) := \Big\{\mu \ll \mathrm{Vol} \;:\; \mu(\mathbb{R}^n) = 1, \;\; \textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}|x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu < \infty\Big\}$$

An optimal transport map from $\mu_0$ to $\mu_1$ is a Borel map $\mathrm{T}$ attaining $$W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1) \;=\; \min\Big\{\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}|\mathrm{T}x - x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x) \;:\; \mathrm{T}_\sharp\mu_0 = \mu_1\Big\}.$$

The minimizer is unique, and $\mathrm{T} = \nabla\varphi$ for a convex function $\varphi$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Brenier '91, McCann '95

The displacement interpolation between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ is: $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda := (1-\lambda)\,\mathrm{Id} + \lambda\,\mathrm{T}, \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1.$$

Theorem (McCann '97). $\mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex: for all $\mu_0,\mu_1$ and all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$, $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1],$$ where $\mu_\lambda$ is the displacement interpolation between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$, and $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu] := \int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu \qquad \text{for } \mu = f\,\mathrm{Vol}.$$

Displacement convexity

$(M,g)$ complete Riemannian manifold. $$\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_2(M) := \Big\{\mu \ll \mathrm{Vol}_g \;:\; \mu(M) = 1, \;\; \textstyle\int_M d(o,x)^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x) < \infty\Big\}$$

$$W_2^2(\mu_0,\mu_1) \;=\; \min\Big\{\textstyle\int_M d(x,\mathrm{T}x)^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x) \;:\; \mathrm{T}_\sharp\mu_0 = \mu_1\Big\}$$ Unique minimizer: $\ \mathrm{T}x = \exp_x\!\big(\nabla\varphi(x)\big)$, where $\varphi$ is $\tfrac{d^2}{2}$-concave. McCann '01 $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda x := \exp_x\!\big(\lambda\nabla\varphi(x)\big), \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1.$$

Displacement convexity

$(M,g)$ globally hyperbolic spacetime, $\ 0 < q < 1$. $$\ell(x,y) := \sup\Big\{\textstyle\int_0^1 |\dot\gamma|\,\mathrm{d}t \;:\; \gamma \text{ future-directed causal},\ \gamma(0) = x,\ \gamma(1) = y\Big\}, \qquad x,y \in M.$$

$\mu_0,\mu_1$ absolutely continuous and compactly supported, with $x_0 \ll x_1$ for all $(x_0,x_1) \in \operatorname{supp}\mu_0 \times \operatorname{supp}\mu_1$ ($x_0 \ll x_1$: there is a future-directed timelike curve from $x_0$ to $x_1$).

$$\ell_q(\mu_0,\mu_1)^q \;=\; \max\Big\{\textstyle\int_M \ell(x,\mathrm{T}x)^q\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x) \;:\; \mathrm{T}_\sharp\mu_0 = \mu_1\Big\}$$ Unique maximizer: $\ \mathrm{T}x = \exp_x\!\big(\nabla\varphi(x)\big)$. $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda x := \exp_x\!\big(\lambda\nabla\varphi(x)\big), \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1.$$

Theorem (McCann '20, Mondino & Suhr '22, Cavalletti & Mondino '24)

The following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric}(v,v) \ge 0$ for every unit timelike $v$  (the strong energy condition).

$(ii)$ $\ \mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex along every displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)_{0\le\lambda\le1}$: $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1].$$

Theorem (McCann '20, Mondino & Suhr '22, Cavalletti & Mondino '24)

Let ${\color{#b3261e} k} \in \mathbb{R}$. The following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric}(v,v) \ge {\color{#b3261e} k}$ for every unit timelike $v$.

$(ii)$ $\ \mathrm{Ent}$ is ${\color{#b3261e} k}$-displacement convex along every displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)_{0\le\lambda\le1}$: $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1] {\color{#b3261e} {} - \tfrac{k}{2}\cdot\lambda(1-\lambda)\cdot \textstyle\int_M \ell(x,\mathrm{T}x)^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x)}.$$

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

$(M,g)$: $n$-dimensional Lorentzian manifold of signature $(+ - \cdots -)$, time-oriented, i.e. endowed with a timelike vector field $Z$.

$v \in TM$ is timelike if $\langle v,v\rangle > 0$, null if $\langle v,v\rangle = 0$ and $v \ne 0$, and causal if it is timelike or null.

A causal vector $v$ is future-directed if $\langle Z,v\rangle > 0$. A curve is timelike, causal or future-directed if its velocity is.

$x_0 \ll x_1$ if there is a future-directed timelike curve joining $x_0$ to $x_1$.

$(M,g)$ is globally hyperbolic if there are no closed causal curves and, for all $x,y \in M$, the set $\{z \in M : x \ll z\} \cap \{z \in M : z \ll y\}$ is precompact. Equivalently, $M$ admits a Cauchy hypersurface: a spacelike hypersurface met exactly once by every inextendible timelike curve.

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

$A$ one-form on $M$, $$F := \mathrm{d}A.$$

A test particle of unit charge and mass moving in the electromagnetic field $F$ is described by a unit-speed timelike curve $\gamma$ solving the Lorentz force equation $$\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = F^\sharp\dot\gamma,$$ where $F^\sharp$ is the $(1,1)$-tensor defined by $\langle\,\cdot\,,F^\sharp\,\cdot\,\rangle = F$.

A solution $u$ of the Hamilton–Jacobi equation $$|\mathrm{d}u + A|_g = 1,$$ with $\mathrm{d}u + A$ past-directed, describes a flow of charged test particles: $\ V := -\nabla u - A^\sharp$ is future-directed, $|V| \equiv 1$, and solves $\nabla_V V = F^\sharp V$.

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

Cost function: $$\mathrm{c}(x,y) := \inf_\gamma \int_\gamma\bigl(-|\dot\gamma| - A(\dot\gamma)\bigr),$$ the infimum among all future-directed timelike curves joining $x$ to $y$.

Electromagnetic Lagrangian, for $r \in (0,1)$: $$L(v) := \begin{cases} -\frac1r|v|^r + \frac1r - 1 - A(v), & v \text{ is future-directed or } v = 0,\\ \infty, & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

By considering free-time rather than fixed-time minimizers of the action, we force the minimizers to have unit speed and to minimize the parametrization-invariant functional $\int -|\dot\gamma| - A(\dot\gamma)$.

McCann '18, Braun, Gigli, McCann, Ohanyan & Sämann '26

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

$\Phi$: the flow map of the Lorentz force equation.

A displacement interpolation between $\mu_0,\mu_1$: $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda := \pi\circ\Phi\circ(V_0,\lambda\ell), \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1,$$ where

  • $V_0$ is a vector field on $\operatorname{supp}\mu_0$,
  • $\ell \ge 0$ is a function on $\operatorname{supp}\mu_0$ (the duration function),
  • $\mathrm{T}_1$ is an optimal transport map from $\mu_0$ to $\mu_1$.

$\dot\mu_\lambda := (\Phi\circ(V_0,\lambda\ell))_\sharp\mu_0$.

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

Monotone: for all $x,y$ and all $\tau \ge 0$, $$\Phi(V_0\vert_x,\tau) = V_0\vert_y \quad\implies\quad \ell(x) \le \ell(y) + \tau.$$

If a solution to the Lorentz force equation issuing from $x$ at velocity $V_0\vert_x$ passes through $y$ after time $\tau$, then it must terminate no later than the trajectory issuing from $y$ at velocity $V_0\vert_y$.

$\mathscr{P}_\ll$: pairs $(\mu_0,\mu_1)$ of compactly supported, absolutely continuous Borel probability measures such that $x_0 \ll x_1$ for all $(x_0,x_1) \in \operatorname{supp}\mu_0\times\operatorname{supp}\mu_1$.

$\mathscr{P}_A \subseteq \mathscr{P}_\ll$: pairs for which no null geodesic joining a point of $\operatorname{supp}\mu_0$ to a point of $\operatorname{supp}\mu_1$ is action-minimizing. The two classes coincide in Minkowski spacetime.

For every $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_A$ there exists a unique monotone displacement interpolation between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$. Assouline & Manini '26

Based on the existence of action-minimizing solutions of the Lorentz force equation joining given pairs of points: Minguzzi & Sánchez '06

cf. Feldman & McCann '02, Bernard & Buffoni '06

Electromagnetic Ricci curvature

$$\mathrm{Ric}_F := \mathrm{Ric} + \delta F + \tfrac14\langle F,F\rangle - \tfrac12 F^2$$

A function on future-directed unit tangent vectors. Here $\mathrm{Ric}$ is the Ricci curvature of the metric $g$, $\delta$ is the codifferential, and $F^2 := \langle\,\cdot\,,F^\sharp F^\sharp\,\cdot\,\rangle$.

Kaluza–Klein metric on $\tilde M := M\times S^1$: $\ \tilde g = g - (\mathrm{d}\varphi - A)^2$.

For every future-directed unit vector $V$, $$\mathrm{Ric}_F(V) = \mathrm{Ric}_{\tilde g}(\tilde V,\tilde V), \qquad \text{where} \qquad \tilde V := V + (1 + A(V))\partial_\varphi.$$

Choquet-Bruhat '09, Williams '15

Physical interpretation of $\mathrm{Ric}_F$

Suppose that $n = 4$ and that $M$ is oriented.

$\mathbf{E}_V := F^\sharp V$ and $\mathbf{B}_V := (\star F)^\sharp V$ are the electric and magnetic fields measured by the observer $V$.

$\rho_V := -\delta F(V)$ is the charge density measured by the observer $V$.

$$\mathrm{Ric}_F(V) = \mathrm{Ric}(V,V) - \rho_V + \frac12|\mathbf{B}_V|^2 - |\mathbf{E}_V|^2$$

Main result

Theorem (Assouline & Manini '26). Let $(M,g)$ be a globally hyperbolic, time-oriented Lorentzian manifold, let $A$ be a one-form on $M$, and let $F = \mathrm{d}A$. Then for every relatively open set $B$ of future-directed unit tangent vectors, the following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric}_F \ge 0$ on the set $B$.

$(ii)$ $\ \mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)$ with $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_A$ for which $\dot\mu_\lambda(B) = 1$ for all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$.

$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu] := \int_M \log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ for $\mu = f\,\mathrm{Vol}_g$.

$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1]$ for all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$.

Main result

Theorem (Assouline & Manini '26). Let $(M,g)$ be a globally hyperbolic, time-oriented Lorentzian manifold, let $A$ be a one-form on $M$, and let $F = \mathrm{d}A$. Then for every relatively open set $B$ of future-directed unit tangent vectors and every $k \in \mathbb{R}$, the following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric}_F \ge k$ on the set $B$.

$(ii)$ $\ \mathrm{Ent}$ is $k$-displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)$ with $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_A$ for which $\dot\mu_\lambda(B) = 1$ for all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$.

$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu] := \int_M \log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ for $\mu = f\,\mathrm{Vol}_g$.

$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1] - \frac{k}{2}\cdot\lambda(1-\lambda)\cdot\int_M\ell^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0$ for all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$,

where $\ell$ is the duration function of $(\mu_\lambda)$.

Remarks

  • When $A = 0$, the theorem recovers (the $\ell_1$ version of) the characterization of the strong energy condition via displacement convexity. McCann '20, Cavalletti & Mondino '20
  • Although our Lagrangian contains the term $|\,\cdot\,|_g^r$ with $r \in (0,1)$, the cost $\mathrm{c}$ corresponds to the cost $\ell_1$, rather than $\ell_r$, in Lorentzian optimal transport. Cavalletti & Mondino '20, Braun & McCann '24, Suhr '18

Pointwise lower bounds on $\mathrm{Ric}_F$

Imposing $\mathrm{Ric}_F(v) \ge k$ for all future-directed unit vectors $v$ can be quite restrictive.

Indeed, if $\dim M \ge 3$ and $g$ is Ricci flat then $\mathrm{Ric}_F$ is not bounded from below on future-directed unit vectors unless $F \equiv 0$.

Lemma. Suppose that $n \ge 3$ and there exists $x \in M$ such that $\mathrm{Ric}\vert_x = 0$ and $F\vert_x \ne 0$. Then $\inf\mathrm{Ric}_F = -\infty$ over future-directed unit vectors $v \in T_xM$.

explanation: fast observers see arbitrarily strong fields.

The Raychaudhuri equation

Hamilton–Jacobi equation: $$|\mathrm{d}u + A| = 1 \qquad \text{and } \mathrm{d}u + A \text{ is past-directed.}$$

$V := -\nabla u - A^\sharp$ is future-directed, $|V| \equiv 1$, and solves the Lorentz force equation $$\nabla_V V = F^\sharp V.$$

The Raychaudhuri equation

Kinematic decomposition: $$\nabla V = \sigma + \omega + \frac{\theta}{n-1}\left(\mathrm{Id} - V^\flat\otimes V\right) + V^\flat\otimes F^\sharp V$$

  • the shear $\sigma$ is a traceless self-adjoint $(1,1)$-tensor satisfying $\sigma V = 0$,
  • the rotation $\omega$ is an anti-self-adjoint $(1,1)$-tensor satisfying $\omega V = 0$,
  • the expansion $\theta$ is the divergence of $V$: $\ \theta = \operatorname{div} V$.

Raychaudhuri equation: $$\dot\theta = -\frac{\theta^2}{n-1} - \langle\sigma,\sigma\rangle + \langle\omega,\omega\rangle - \mathrm{Ric}(V,V) + \operatorname{div}(\nabla_V V), \qquad \dot\theta = V\operatorname{div}V.$$

$$\color{#b3261e}{2\omega = P_{V^\perp}F^\sharp P_{V^\perp}}, \qquad 2\langle\omega,\omega\rangle = \tfrac12\langle F,F\rangle + F^2(V,V),$$

where $P_{V^\perp} := \mathrm{Id} - V^\flat\otimes V$.

Electromagnetic Bochner inequality

Let $u$ be a smooth solution to the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, i.e. $$\langle V,V\rangle = 1, \qquad \text{where} \qquad V = -\nabla u - A^\sharp.$$

Then

$$V\operatorname{div}V + \frac{(\operatorname{div}V)^2}{n-1} + \mathrm{Ric}_F(V) \le 0.$$

Electromagnetic fields in Minkowski spacetime

$M := \mathbb{R}^{1+3}$, $\quad g = \mathrm{dt}^2 - \mathrm{dx}^2 - \mathrm{dy}^2 - \mathrm{dz}^2.$

A general electromagnetic field takes the form

$$F = \mathrm{dt}\wedge(E^\mathrm{x}\,\mathrm{dx} + E^\mathrm{y}\,\mathrm{dy} + E^\mathrm{z}\,\mathrm{dz}) - B^\mathrm{x}\,\mathrm{dy}\wedge\mathrm{dz} + B^\mathrm{y}\,\mathrm{dx}\wedge\mathrm{dz} - B^\mathrm{z}\,\mathrm{dx}\wedge\mathrm{dy}.$$

The electric and magnetic fields are the vector fields $$\mathbf{E} = E^\mathrm{x}\partial_\mathrm{x} + E^\mathrm{y}\partial_\mathrm{y} + E^\mathrm{z}\partial_\mathrm{z} \quad\text{and}\quad \mathbf{B} = B^\mathrm{x}\partial_\mathrm{x} + B^\mathrm{y}\partial_\mathrm{y} + B^\mathrm{z}\partial_\mathrm{z}.$$

Current: $J = -\delta F$.

Displacement convexity in a source-free magnetic field

The speed of a future-directed unit vector $v \in T_xM$ with respect to the observer $Z$: $\sqrt{1 - \langle v,Z\vert_x\rangle^{-2}}$, i.e. the ratio of the spatial to the temporal component of $v$ in any orthonormal basis $Z\vert_x,e_1,\dots,e_{n-1}$.

Theorem. Let $(M,g)$ be Minkowski spacetime, and suppose that $F = \mathrm{d}A$ is purely magnetic with respect to $\partial_\mathrm{t}$, and source-free: $$\iota_{\partial_\mathrm{t}}F \equiv 0, \qquad \delta F \equiv 0.$$ Then $\mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)$ with $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_\ll$ such that $\dot\mu_0$-almost every $v$ has speed less than $1/\sqrt2$ with respect to the observer $\partial_\mathrm{t}$.

The speed bound $1/\sqrt2$ is the same for any source-free magnetic field.

$$\mathrm{Ric}_F(v) = \tfrac12\left(|\mathbf{B}|^2 - |\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{v}|^2\right), \qquad v = v^\mathrm{t}\partial_\mathrm{t} + \mathbf{v}.$$

Speed less than $1/\sqrt2$: $\ |\mathbf{v}| \lt 1$, $\ $ so $\ |\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{v}| \le |\mathbf{B}|\,|\mathbf{v}| \le |\mathbf{B}|$.

Displacement convexity detects the magnitude of point charges

Theorem. Let $(M,g) := (\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}),\ \mathrm{dt}^2 - \mathrm{dx}^2 - \mathrm{dy}^2 - \mathrm{dz}^2)$, let $q_0 \in \mathbb{R}$, and consider the Coulomb potential and field $$A = \frac{q_0}{r}\,\mathrm{dt} \qquad\text{and}\qquad F = \mathrm{d}A = -\frac{q_0}{r^2}\,\mathrm{d}r\wedge\mathrm{dt}, \qquad \text{where } r = \sqrt{\mathrm{x}^2 + \mathrm{y}^2 + \mathrm{z}^2}.$$

For a tangent vector $v$, write $\mathbf{v}_\perp := v - \mathrm{dt}(v)\,\partial_\mathrm{t} - \mathrm{d}r(v)\,\partial_r$ for the spatial component of $v$ orthogonal to $\partial_r$, and denote by $v_\perp$ the Euclidean norm of $\mathbf{v}_\perp$. For $r_1 > 0$ and $\sigma > 0$, write $U_{r_1} := \{r > r_1\}$ and $B_{r_1,\sigma}$ the set of future-directed unit vectors $v$ based in $U_{r_1}$ with $v_\perp \lt \sigma$.

Then for every $Q \ge 0$, the following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ |q_0| \le Q$.

$(ii)$ $\ \mathrm{Ent}$ is $k(Q,r_1,\sigma)$-displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)$ with $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_\ll$ such that $\dot\mu_\lambda(B_{r_1,\sigma}) = 1$ for all $\lambda \in [0,1]$, where $$k(Q,r_1,\sigma) = -\frac{Q^2}{r_1^4}\left(1 + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right).$$

Displacement convexity detects the magnitude of point charges

$$\mathrm{Ric}_F(v) = -|\mathbf{E}|^2 - \tfrac12|\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{v}|^2 = -\frac{q_0^2}{r^4}\left(1 + \frac{v_\perp^2}{2}\right) \qquad \text{for } v \in B_{r_1,\sigma}.$$

Remark. For $Q = 0$, $(i)\Rightarrow(ii)$ recovers (the $\ell^1$ version of) displacement convexity for timelike optimal transport in Minkowski spacetime, while $(ii)\Rightarrow(i)$ shows that for any nonzero background point charge, displacement convexity breaks down.

Charge-to-mass ratio

Multiplying the field $F$ by a parameter $s \in \mathbb{R}$ has the same effect on the equations of motion as multiplying the charge of the transported particles by $s$ while keeping the field $F$ fixed.

Displacement interpolations for the charge-to-mass ratio $s$: displacement interpolations corresponding to the electromagnetic potential $sA$.

If $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_\ll$ then $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_{sA}$ for all sufficiently small $|s|$, so there exists a unique monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu^s_\lambda)$ for the charge-to-mass ratio $s$ joining $\mu_0$ to $\mu_1$.

Assouline & Manini '26, based on Minguzzi & Sánchez '06; for Dirac measures: Caponio & Minguzzi '04

Displacement convexity detects the sign of the charge density

Theorem. Suppose that $(M,g)$ is globally hyperbolic and time-oriented and that $g$ is Ricci flat: $\ \mathrm{Ric} \equiv 0$.

Let $U \subseteq M$ be a precompact open set and assume that the current $J = -\delta F$ is timelike on the closure of $U$: $\ \langle J,J\rangle > 0$ on $\overline U$.

Fix a continuous future-directed unit timelike vector field $Z$ on $\overline U$. Then the following are equivalent:

$(i)$ The current $J$ is future-directed (resp. past-directed) on $\overline U$.

$(ii)$ There exist $0 \lt \sigma \lt 1$ and $s_0 > 0$ such that for all $-s_0 \lt s \le 0$ (resp. $0 \le s \lt s_0$), $\mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)_{0\le\lambda\le1}$ for the charge-to-mass ratio $s$ such that $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_{sA}$ and such that $\mu_\lambda(U) = 1$ and $\dot\mu_\lambda$-almost every $v$ has speed less than $\sigma$ with respect to the observer $Z$ for every $0 \le \lambda \le 1$.

$$\mathrm{Ric}_{sF}(v) = -s\,J(v) - s^2\left(\tfrac12F^2 - \tfrac14\langle F,F\rangle\right)(v).$$

Needle decomposition

In the case of $L^1$ (Monge–Kantorovich) optimal transport, the manifold decomposes into disjoint action-minimizing trajectories, and by transporting mass along each such trajectory one obtains an optimal transport map. Evans & Gangbo '99, Caffarelli, Feldman & McCann '02, Feldman & McCann '02, Bernard & Buffoni '06, Figalli '07, Fathi & Figalli '10

This can be used to reduce geometric inequalities on weighted manifolds to their one-dimensional counterparts: needle decomposition. Klartag '17

Inspired by the localization technique in convex geometry: Gromov & Milman '87, Kannan, Lovász & Simonovits '95.
Extended to: Finsler, Ohta '18; metric measure spaces, Cavalletti & Mondino '17; sub-Riemannian, Milman '21; Lorentzian, Cavalletti & Mondino '20, Braun & McCann '24; Tonelli Lagrangians, Assouline '24.

Needle decomposition

$\mu_0 = f_0\,\mathrm{Vol}_g$, $\mu_1 = f_1\,\mathrm{Vol}_g$. A family $\Gamma$ of minimizing extremals, a probability measure $\nu$ on $\Gamma$, and measures $\mathfrak{m}_\gamma$:

  • $\displaystyle \mathrm{Vol}_g = \int_\Gamma \mathfrak{m}_\gamma\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\gamma)$;
  • $\mathfrak{m}_\gamma = \gamma_\sharp(m_\gamma)$, where $m_\gamma$ has a smooth, positive density $e^{-\psi_\gamma}$;
  • $\displaystyle \int f_0\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_\gamma = \int f_1\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_\gamma$;
  • a description of the densities of the needles in terms of solutions to the Hamilton–Jacobi equation.

Together with the Bochner inequality, the assumption $\mathrm{Ric}_F \ge k$ implies a curvature-dimension condition for the needles, and the rest of the proof proceeds as in previous works. Klartag '17, Ohta '17, Cavalletti & Mondino '20, Assouline '24

Extended-real-valued Lagrangians

Besides attaining the value $\infty$, the Lagrangian fails to grow superlinearly at infinity, a condition required of Tonelli Lagrangians.

Needle decomposition for extended-real-valued Lagrangians: Assouline & Manini '26

  • replace $L$ locally, in a neighborhood of each point of the transport set, by a Tonelli Lagrangian which coincides with $L$ in the direction of the transport rays;
  • apply the needle decomposition result for Tonelli Lagrangians (Assouline '24) to each local Lagrangian;
  • glue together the local needle decompositions to obtain a global one.

Weighted manifolds

$\mathfrak{m} = e^{-\psi}\mathrm{Vol}_g$, $\qquad \delta_\mathfrak{m}F = \delta F + \iota_{\nabla\psi}F$,

$$\mathrm{Ric}_{F,\mathfrak{m}} := \mathrm{Ric} + \mathrm{Hess}\,\psi + \delta_\mathfrak{m}F + \tfrac14\langle F,F\rangle - \tfrac12F^2.$$

Theorem. Let $(M,g)$ be a globally hyperbolic, time-oriented Lorentzian manifold, let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a measure on $M$ with a smooth density, let $A$ be a one-form on $M$, and let $F = \mathrm{d}A$. For every relatively open set $B$ of future-directed unit tangent vectors and every $k \in \mathbb{R}$, the following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric}_{F,\mathfrak{m}} \ge k$ on the set $B$.

$(ii)$ $\ \mathrm{Ent}[\,\cdot\,|\mathfrak{m}]$ is $k$-displacement convex with respect to $\mathfrak{m}$ along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)_{0\le\lambda\le1}$ with $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_A$ such that $\dot\mu_\lambda(B) = 1$ for all $\lambda \in [0,1]$.

$\mathrm{Ric}_{F,\mathfrak{m}} \ge K$ is an electromagnetic analogue of the $\mathsf{CD}(K,\infty)$ condition of Lott–Sturm–Villani, and is closely related to the Bakry–Émery curvature-dimension criterion. Bakry & Émery '85
