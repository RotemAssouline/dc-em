Displacement convexity in $\mathbb{R}^n$

  • An optimal transport map between two probability measures $\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is a Borel map $\mathrm{T}$ attaining $$\min\Big\{\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}|\mathrm{T}x - x|^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x) \;:\; \mathrm{T}_\sharp\mu_0 = \mu_1\Big\}.$$

    $\mathscr{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^n):=$ absolutely continuous probability measures on $\mathbb{R}^n$ with finite second moment.

  • The minimizer is unique, and given by $\mathrm{T} = \nabla\varphi$ for a convex function $\varphi$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Brenier '91, McCann '95
  • The displacement interpolation between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ is: $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda := (1-\lambda)\,\mathrm{Id} + \lambda\,\mathrm{T}, \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1.$$

Theorem (McCann '97). For all $\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_2(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$, $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1],$$ where $\mu_\lambda$ is the displacement interpolation between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$, and $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu] := \int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu \qquad \text{for } \mu = f\,\mathrm{Vol}.$$

Displacement convexity on Riemannian manifolds

$(M,g)$ complete Riemannian manifold.

  • An optimal transport map between two probability measures $\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_2(M)$ is a Borel map $\mathrm{T}$ attaining $$\min\Big\{\textstyle\int_M d(x,\mathrm{T}x)^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x) \;:\; \mathrm{T}_\sharp\mu_0 = \mu_1\Big\}.$$

    $\mathscr{P}_2(M):=$ absolutely continuous probability measures on $M$ with finite second moment.

  • The minimizer is unique, and given by $\mathrm{T}x = \exp_x\!\big(\nabla\varphi\vert_x\big)$ for a $\tfrac{d^2}{2}$-concave function $\varphi$ on $M$. McCann '01
  • The displacement interpolation between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ is: $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda x := \exp_x\!\big(\lambda\nabla\varphi\vert_x\big), \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1.$$

Theorem (Cordero-Erausquin, McCann & Schmuckenschläger '01, von Renesse & Sturm '05, Sturm '06, Lott & Villani '09)

The following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric} \ge 0$.

$(ii)$ $\ $For all $\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_2(M)$ and all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$, $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1],$$

where $\mathrm{Ent}[\mu] := \textstyle\int_M\log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ for $\mu = f\,\mathrm{Vol}_g$.

Theorem (Cordero-Erausquin, McCann & Schmuckenschläger '01, von Renesse & Sturm '05, Sturm '06, Lott & Villani '09)

Let ${\color{#d9730d} k} \in \mathbb{R}$. The following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric} \ge {\color{#d9730d} k}$.

$(ii)$ $\ $For all $\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_2(M)$ and all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$, $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1] {\color{#d9730d} {} - \tfrac{k}{2}\cdot\lambda(1-\lambda)\cdot \textstyle\int_M d(x,\mathrm{T}x)^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x)},$$

where $\mathrm{Ent}[\mu] := \textstyle\int_M\log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ for $\mu = f\,\mathrm{Vol}_g$.

Displacement convexity in $L^1$ optimal transport

$(M,g)$ complete Riemannian manifold

  • An ${\color{#2e7d32} L^1}$-optimal transport map between two probability measures $\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_1(M)$ is a Borel map $\mathrm{T}$ attaining $$\min\Big\{\textstyle\int_M {\color{#2e7d32} d(x,\mathrm{T}x)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x) \;:\; \mathrm{T}_\sharp\mu_0 = \mu_1\Big\}.$$

    $\mathscr{P}_1(M):=$ absolutely continuous probability measures on $M$ with finite first moment.

  • A minimizer exists, and every minimizer has the form $\mathrm{T}x = \exp_x\!\big(\ell(x)\nabla u\vert_x\big)$ for a 1-Lipschitz function $u$ and a measurable function $\ell$. Evans & Gangbo '99, Feldman & McCann '02, Caffarelli, Feldman & McCann '02
  • An $L^1$ displacement interpolation between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ is: $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda x := \exp_x\!\big(\lambda\,\ell(x)\nabla u\vert_x\big), \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1.$$

Theorem (Cavalletti & Milman '21, Cavalletti, Gigli & Santarcangelo '21)

Let $k \in \mathbb{R}$. The following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric} \ge k$.

$(ii)$ $\ $For all $\mu_0,\mu_1 \in \mathscr{P}_1(M)$ there exists an $L^1$ displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)_{0\le\lambda\le1}$ along which, for all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$, $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1] - \tfrac{k}{2}\cdot\lambda(1-\lambda)\cdot \textstyle\int_M \ell^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0.$$

Displacement convexity on Lorentzian manifolds

$(M,g)$ globally hyperbolic Lorentzian manifold $$\tau(x,y) := \sup\Big\{\textstyle\int_0^1 |\dot\gamma|\,\mathrm{d}t \;:\; \gamma \text{ future-directed},\ \gamma(0) = x,\ \gamma(1) = y\Big\}, \qquad x \ll y.$$

  • For $0 < q < 1$, an $\ell^q$-optimal transport map between two probability measures $\mu_0,\mu_1$ is a Borel map $\mathrm{T}$ attaining $$\max\Big\{\textstyle\int_M \tau(x,\mathrm{T}x)^q\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x) \;:\; \mathrm{T}_\sharp\mu_0 = \mu_1\Big\}.$$
  • If $\mu_0,\mu_1$ are a.c. and compactly supported, with $x_0 \ll x_1$ for all $(x_0,x_1) \in \operatorname{supp}\mu_0 \times \operatorname{supp}\mu_1$, then a maximizer exists, is unique, and is given by $\mathrm{T}x = \exp_x\!\big(\nabla\varphi\vert_x\big)$ for a $\tau^q$-concave function $\varphi$ on $M$. McCann '20, Cavalletti & Mondino '24
  • The displacement interpolation between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ is: $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda x := \exp_x\!\big(\lambda\nabla\varphi\vert_x\big), \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1.$$

Theorem (McCann '20, Suhr '18, Mondino & Suhr '22, Braun '23, Braun & McCann '24, Cavalletti & Mondino '24)

Let $k \in \mathbb{R}$. The following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric}(v,v) \ge k$ for every unit timelike $v$.

$(ii)$ $\ $For all $\mu_0,\mu_1$ as above and all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$, $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1] - \tfrac{k}{2}\cdot\lambda(1-\lambda)\cdot \textstyle\int_M \tau(x,\mathrm{T}x)^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x).$$

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

Background: Lorentzian geometry

  • $(M,g)$: $n$-dimensional Lorentzian manifold, i.e. $g = \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is a symmetric $(2,0)$-tensor on $M$ of signature $(+ - \cdots -)$.
  • $v \in TM$ is timelike if $\langle v,v\rangle > 0$, and null if $\langle v,v\rangle = 0$, $v \ne 0$.
  • $M$ is time-oriented, i.e. endowed with a timelike vector field $Z$.
  • A timelike or null $v$ is future-directed if $\langle Z,v\rangle > 0$.
  • A curve $\gamma$ is timelike or future-directed if $\dot\gamma(t)$ is, for all $t$.
  • $x_0 \ll x_1$ if a future-directed timelike curve joins $x_0$ to $x_1$.
  • $M$ is globally hyperbolic, i.e. there are no closed future-directed curves and every chronological diamond $\{z \in M : x \ll z\} \cap \{z \in M : z \ll y\}$ is precompact.
    Equivalently, $M \cong \mathbb{R} \times M'$ with $g = \beta\,\mathrm{d}t^2 - h_t$, where $\beta : M \to (0,\infty)$ is a smooth function and $h_t$ is a smooth family of Riemannian metrics on $M'$.

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

Background: electromagnetic fields

$(M,g)$ Lorentzian manifold, $A \in \Omega^1(M)$ one-form on $M$, $$F := \mathrm{d}A.$$

If $M$ oriented and $\dim M = 4$, and $V$ is a future-directed unit vector field (an observer), then the electric and magnetic fields measured by \(V\) are $$\mathbf{E}_V := F^\sharp V, \qquad \mathbf{B}_V := (\star F)^\sharp V,$$ where $F^\sharp$ is the $(1,1)$-tensor defined by $\langle\,\cdot\,,F^\sharp\,\cdot\,\rangle = F$.

A test particle of unit charge and mass moving in the electromagnetic field $F$ is described by a unit-speed timelike curve $\gamma$ solving the Lorentz force equation $$\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = F^\sharp\dot\gamma. \tag{LFE}$$

A future-directed timelike unit speed curve $\gamma$ minimizing the action $$\int_\gamma\bigl(-|\dot\gamma| - A(\dot\gamma)\bigr)$$ is a solution to the Lorentz force equation.

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

Background: charges and currents

The codifferential of $F$ is $$\delta F = \star^{-1}\mathrm{d}\star F = -\operatorname{div} F^\sharp,$$ where $\star$ is the Hodge star.

The current is the one-form $$J := -\delta F.$$ The field is source-free if $$\delta F = 0.$$

Given an observer $V$, write $$J^\sharp = \rho_V\,V + \mathbf{j}_V, \qquad \mathbf{j}_V \perp V, \qquad \rho_V := J(V),$$ where $\rho_V$ is the charge density and $\mathbf{j}_V$ the current density measured by $V$. In Minkowski spacetime with $V = \partial_\mathrm{t}$: $$\rho = \operatorname{div}_{\mathbb{R}^3}(\mathbf{E}), \qquad \mathbf{j} = \mathrm{curl}_{\mathbb{R}^3}(\mathbf{B}) - \partial_\mathrm{t}\mathbf{E}.$$

Background charges are described by $J$ and generate the field $F$ through Maxwell's equations $$\mathrm{d}F = 0, \qquad \delta F = -J.$$ Test particles move in the field $F$ according to (LFE), but do not affect $F$.

Optimal transport with electromagnetic cost

Background: the electromagnetic cost function

Cost function: $$\mathrm{c}(x,y) := \inf_\gamma \int_\gamma\bigl(-|\dot\gamma| - A(\dot\gamma)\bigr),$$ where the infimum is over all future-directed timelike curves joining $x$ to $y$.

For every $r \in (0,1)$, timelike curves attaining the infimum and parametrized by unit speed, are exactly free-time minimizers of the action $\int L(\dot\gamma)$, where $L$ is the Lagrangian $$L(v) := \begin{cases} -\frac1r|v|^r + \frac1r - 1 - A(v), & v \text{ is future-directed or } v = 0,\\ \infty, & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

Displacement interpolations

For $v \in TM$, denote by $\gamma_v$ the solution to (LFE) with $\dot\gamma_v(0) = v$.

A displacement interpolation between $\mu_0,\mu_1$ is a family of probability measures $(\mu_\lambda)_{0\le\lambda\le1}$ of the form $$\mu_\lambda = (\mathrm{T}_\lambda)_\sharp\mu_0, \qquad \mathrm{T}_\lambda x := \gamma_{V_0\vert_x}\big(\lambda\,\ell(x)\big), \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1,$$ where

  • $V_0$ is a vector field on $\operatorname{supp}\mu_0$,
  • $\ell \ge 0$ is a function on $\operatorname{supp}\mu_0$ (the duration function),
  • $\mathrm{T} := \mathrm{T}_1$ is an optimal transport map from $\mu_0$ to $\mu_1$, i.e. $\mathrm{T}$ attains $$\min\Big\{\textstyle\int_M \mathrm{c}(x,\mathrm{T}x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0(x) \;:\; \mathrm{T}_\sharp\mu_0 = \mu_1\Big\}.$$

Write $\dmu_\lambda$ for the law of the velocities $\dot\gamma_{V_0\vert_x}\big(\lambda\,\ell(x)\big)$, $x \sim \mu_0$, so that $\mu_\lambda = \pi_\sharp\dmu_\lambda$.

Displacement interpolations

$\mathscr{P}_\ll$: pairs $(\mu_0,\mu_1)$ of compactly supported, absolutely continuous Borel probability measures such that $x_0 \ll x_1$ for all $(x_0,x_1) \in \operatorname{supp}\mu_0\times\operatorname{supp}\mu_1$.

$\mathscr{P}_A \subseteq \mathscr{P}_\ll$: pairs for which no null geodesic joining a point of $\operatorname{supp}\mu_0$ to a point of $\operatorname{supp}\mu_1$ is action-minimizing. $\mathscr{P}_A = \mathscr{P}_\ll$ in Minkowski space.

Proposition (A. & Manini '26). Let $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_A$. Then there is a unique monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)_{0 \le \lambda \le 1}$ between $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$, and the pair $(V_0,\ell)$ inducing it is determined $\mu_0$-almost everywhere.

Proposition (A. & Manini '26). Let $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_\ll$. There exists $s_1 > 0$ such that for every $s \in (-s_1,s_1)$, the pair $(\mu_0,\mu_1)$ lies in $\mathscr{P}_{sA}$.

Action-minimizing solutions of (LFE) joining given pairs of points: Minguzzi & Sánchez '06.

Electromagnetic Ricci curvature

$SM := \{\text{future-directed unit vectors}\}$

$$\mathrm{Ric}_F : SM \to \mathbb{R}, \qquad \mathrm{Ric}_F := \mathrm{Ric} + \delta F + \tfrac14\langle F,F\rangle - \tfrac12 F^2$$ where $F^2 := \langle\,\cdot\,,F^\sharp F^\sharp\,\cdot\,\rangle$.

If $M$ is oriented and $\dim M = 4$, then for every $V \in SM$, $$\mathrm{Ric}_F(V) = \mathrm{Ric}(V,V) - \rho_V + \tfrac12|\mathbf{B}_V|^2 - |\mathbf{E}_V|^2.$$

Kaluza–Klein metric on $\tilde M := M\times S^1$: $\ \tilde g = g - (\mathrm{d}\varphi - A)^2$.

For every future-directed unit vector $V$, $$\mathrm{Ric}_F(V) = \mathrm{Ric}_{\tilde g}(\tilde V,\tilde V), \qquad \text{where} \qquad \tilde V := V + (1 + A(V))\partial_\varphi.$$

Main result

$$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu] := \int_M \log f\,\mathrm{d}\mu \qquad \text{for } \mu = f\,\mathrm{Vol}_g.$$

For $k \in \mathbb{R}$, we say that $\mathrm{Ent}$ is $k$-displacement convex along a monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)$ if, $$\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_\lambda] \le (1-\lambda)\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_0] + \lambda\,\mathrm{Ent}[\mu_1] - \tfrac{k}{2}\cdot\lambda(1-\lambda)\cdot \textstyle\int_M\ell^2\,\mathrm{d}\mu_0, \qquad 0 \le \lambda \le 1,$$ where $\ell$ is the duration function. We say that $\mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex if this holds with $k = 0$.

Theorem (A. & Manini '26). Let $(M,g)$ be a globally hyperbolic, time-oriented Lorentzian manifold, let $A$ be a one-form on $M$, and let $F = \mathrm{d}A$. Then for every relatively open set $B \subseteq SM$, the following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ \mathrm{Ric}_F \ge 0$ on the set $B$.

$(ii)$ $\ \mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)$ between $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_A$ for which $\dmu_\lambda(B) = 1$ for all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$.

Can we require $\mathrm{Ric}_F \ge k$ everywhere?

No.

Lemma. Suppose that $n \ge 3$ and there exists $x \in M$ such that $\mathrm{Ric}\vert_x = 0$ and $F\vert_x \ne 0$. Then $\inf\mathrm{Ric}_F = -\infty$ over future-directed unit vectors $v \in T_xM$.

Proof: For $\mathrm{Ric}\vert_x = 0$ and a future-directed unit vector $V \in \mathrm{T}_xM$, $$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{Ric}_F(V) &= \mathrm{Ric}(V,V) + \delta F(V) + \tfrac14\langle F,F\rangle - \tfrac12 F^2(V,V)\\[2pt] &= \class{ub ub1}{\underbrace{\class{ub-base}{\mathrm{Ric}(V,V)}}_{0}} \qquad\class{ub ub2}{\underbrace{\class{ub-base}{-\ \rho_V}}_{\text{linear in } V}} \qquad\class{ub ub3}{\underbrace{\class{ub-base}{+\ \tfrac14\langle F,F\rangle}}_{\text{observer-independent}}} \qquad\class{circ-term}{-\ \tfrac12|\mathbf{E}_V|^2} \end{aligned}$$

Since $n \ge 3$, there is a future-directed null $w \in \mathrm{T}_xM$ with $F^\sharp w \notin \mathbb{R}w$. Take a future-directed null $z$ with $\langle z,w\rangle = \tfrac12$ and let $V_r := \tfrac1r\,z + r\,w$. Then $V_r$ is a future-directed unit vector and $$ |\mathbf{E}_{V_r}|^2 = F^2(V_r,V_r) = r^2\underbrace{F^2(w,w)}_{-\langle F^\sharp w,F^\sharp w\rangle\,>\,0} + O(1), $$ where $F^2(w,w) > 0$ because $F^\sharp w$ is orthogonal to the null vector $w$ (by skew-adjointness) but not parallel to it, hence spacelike.

Electromagnetic displacement convexity in Minkowski space

$M := \mathbb{R}^{1+3}$, $\quad g = \mathrm{dt}^2 - \mathrm{dx}^2 - \mathrm{dy}^2 - \mathrm{dz}^2.$

$$F = \mathrm{dt}\wedge(E^\mathrm{x}\,\mathrm{dx} + E^\mathrm{y}\,\mathrm{dy} + E^\mathrm{z}\,\mathrm{dz}) - B^\mathrm{x}\,\mathrm{dy}\wedge\mathrm{dz} + B^\mathrm{y}\,\mathrm{dx}\wedge\mathrm{dz} - B^\mathrm{z}\,\mathrm{dx}\wedge\mathrm{dy}.$$

Electric and magnetic fields: $$\mathbf{E} = E^\mathrm{x}\partial_\mathrm{x} + E^\mathrm{y}\partial_\mathrm{y} + E^\mathrm{z}\partial_\mathrm{z} \quad\text{and}\quad \mathbf{B} = B^\mathrm{x}\partial_\mathrm{x} + B^\mathrm{y}\partial_\mathrm{y} + B^\mathrm{z}\partial_\mathrm{z}.$$

Charge and current densities: $$\rho := \operatorname{div}_{\mathbb{R}^3}\mathbf{E} \quad\text{and}\quad \mathbf{j} := \operatorname{curl}_{\mathbb{R}^3}\mathbf{B} - \partial_\mathrm{t}\mathbf{E}.$$

For every future-directed unit vector $v = v^\mathrm{t}\partial_\mathrm{t} + \mathbf{v}$, $$\mathrm{Ric}_F(v) = \tfrac12|\mathbf{B}|^2 - |\mathbf{E}|^2 - \tfrac12|\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{v}|^2 - \tfrac12|\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{v}|^2 + v^\mathrm{t}\langle\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B},\mathbf{v}\rangle - \rho\,v^\mathrm{t} + \langle\mathbf{j},\mathbf{v}\rangle.$$

Displacement convexity in a source-free magnetic field

The speed of a future-directed unit vector $v$ with respect to an observer $Z$ is $\sqrt{1 - \langle v,Z\rangle^{-2}}$.

Theorem (A. & Manini '26). Let $(M,g)$ be Minkowski space, and suppose that $F = \mathrm{d}A$ is purely magnetic and source-free: $$\mathbf{E} \equiv 0, \qquad \rho \equiv 0, \qquad \mathbf{j} \equiv 0.$$ Then $\mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)$ with $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_\ll$ such that $\dmu_0$-almost every $v$ has speed less than $1/\sqrt2$ w.r.t $\partial_\mathrm{t}$.

$$\mathrm{Ric}_F(v) = \tfrac12\left(|\mathbf{B}|^2 - |\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{v}|^2\right), \qquad v = v^\mathrm{t}\partial_\mathrm{t} + \mathbf{v}.$$

Speed less than $1/\sqrt2$ $\implies$ $\ |\mathbf{v}| \lt 1$ $\implies \ |\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{v}| \le |\mathbf{B}|\,|\mathbf{v}| \le |\mathbf{B}|$.

Displacement convexity detects the magnitude of point charges

Theorem (A. & Manini '26). Let $(M,g) := (\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^3\setminus\{0\}),\ \mathrm{dt}^2 - \mathrm{dx}^2 - \mathrm{dy}^2 - \mathrm{dz}^2)$, let $q_0 \in \mathbb{R}$, and consider the Coulomb potential and field $$A = \frac{q_0}{r}\,\mathrm{dt} \qquad\text{and}\qquad F = \mathrm{d}A = -\frac{q_0}{r^2}\,\mathrm{d}r\wedge\mathrm{dt}, \qquad \text{where } r = \sqrt{\mathrm{x}^2 + \mathrm{y}^2 + \mathrm{z}^2}.$$

For a tangent vector $v$, write $\mathbf{v}_\perp := v - \mathrm{dt}(v)\,\partial_\mathrm{t} - \mathrm{d}r(v)\,\partial_r$ for the spatial component of $v$ orthogonal to $\partial_r$, and denote by $v_\perp$ the Euclidean norm of $\mathbf{v}_\perp$. For $r_1 > 0$ and $\sigma > 0$, write $U_{r_1} := \{r > r_1\}$ and $B_{r_1,\sigma}$ the set of future-directed unit vectors $v$ based in $U_{r_1}$ with $v_\perp \lt \sigma$.

Then for every $Q \ge 0$, the following are equivalent:

$(i)$ $\ |q_0| \le Q$.

$(ii)$ $\ \mathrm{Ent}$ is $k(Q,r_1,\sigma)$-displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)$ with $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_\ll$ such that $\dmu_\lambda(B_{r_1,\sigma}) = 1$ for all $\lambda \in [0,1]$, where $$k(Q,r_1,\sigma) = -\frac{Q^2}{r_1^4}\left(1 + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right).$$

$$\mathrm{Ric}_F(v) = -|\mathbf{E}|^2 - \tfrac12|\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{v}|^2 = -\frac{q_0^2}{r^4}\left(1 + \frac{v_\perp^2}{2}\right) \qquad \text{for } v \in B_{r_1,\sigma}.$$

Displacement convexity detects the sign of the charge density

Displacement interpolations for the charge-to-mass ratio $s$ $:=$ displacement interpolations for $sA$.

If $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_\ll$ then $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_{sA}$ for all sufficiently small $|s|$, so there exists a unique monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu^s_\lambda)$ for the charge-to-mass ratio $s$ joining $\mu_0$ to $\mu_1$.

Theorem (A. & Manini '26). Suppose that $(M,g)$ is globally hyperbolic and time-oriented and that $g$ is Ricci flat: $\ \mathrm{Ric} \equiv 0$.

Let $U \subseteq M$ be a precompact open set and assume that the current $J = -\delta F$ is timelike on the closure of $U$: $\ \langle J,J\rangle > 0$ on $\overline U$ (e.g. $J$ is generated by massive particles whose charges all have the same sign).

Fix a continuous future-directed unit vector field $Z$ on $\overline U$. Then the following are equivalent:

$(i)$ The current $J$ is future-directed (resp. past-directed) on $\overline U$.

$(ii)$ There exist $0 \lt \sigma \lt 1$ and $s_0 > 0$ such that for all $-s_0 \lt s \le 0$ (resp. $0 \le s \lt s_0$), $\mathrm{Ent}$ is displacement convex along every monotone displacement interpolation $(\mu_\lambda)_{0\le\lambda\le1}$ for the charge-to-mass ratio $s$ such that $(\mu_0,\mu_1) \in \mathscr{P}_{sA}$ and such that $\mu_\lambda(U) = 1$ and $\dmu_\lambda$-almost every $v$ has speed less than $\sigma$ with respect to the observer $Z$ for every $0 \le \lambda \le 1$.

$$\mathrm{Ric}_{sF}(v) = -s\,J(v) - s^2\left(\tfrac12F^2 - \tfrac14\langle F,F\rangle\right)(v).$$

Electromagnetic Bochner inequality

Hamilton–Jacobi equation: $\ |\mathrm{d}u + A| = 1$ $\ $ and $\mathrm{d}u + A$ is past-directed.

$V := -\nabla u - A^\sharp$ is future-directed, $|V| \equiv 1$, and solves the Lorentz force equation $\nabla_V V = F^\sharp V$.

$$\nabla V = \underbrace{\sigma}_{\substack{\text{shear}\\ \text{self-adjoint, traceless}}} + \underbrace{\omega}_{\substack{\text{rotation}\\ \text{anti-self-adjoint}}} + \underbrace{\theta}_{\substack{\text{expansion}\\ \theta\, =\, \operatorname{div}V}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Id} - V^\flat\otimes V}{n-1} + V^\flat\otimes F^\sharp V$$

Raychaudhuri equation. $$\dot\theta = -\frac{\theta^2}{n-1} - \langle\sigma,\sigma\rangle + \langle\omega,\omega\rangle - \mathrm{Ric}(V,V) + \operatorname{div}(\nabla_V V), \qquad \dot\theta = V\operatorname{div}V.$$

$$2\langle\omega,\omega\rangle = \tfrac12\langle F,F\rangle + F^2(V,V), \qquad \operatorname{div}(\nabla_VV) = \operatorname{div}(F^\sharp V) = -\delta F(V) - \tfrac12\langle F,F\rangle$$

$$\langle\sigma,\sigma\rangle \ge 0 \quad\implies\quad 0 \ge \dot\theta + \frac{\theta^2}{n-1} + \underbrace{\mathrm{Ric}(V,V) + \delta F(V) + \tfrac14\langle F,F\rangle - \tfrac12F^2(V,V)}_{\textstyle\mathrm{Ric}_F(V)}.$$

$$V\operatorname{div}V + \frac{(\operatorname{div}V)^2}{n-1} + \mathrm{Ric}_F(V) \le 0.$$

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold, let $L : TM \to \mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$, $\ \operatorname{dom}L := L^{-1}(\mathbb{R})$. Assume:

  • (I) for every $x \in M$, $\ L\vert_{T_xM}$ is proper, lower semicontinuous and convex;
  • (II) $L$ is smooth on $\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}L$, with positive definite vertical Hessian $D^2L$;
  • (III) $L$ is fiberwise essentially smooth: $\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}L\cap T_xM \ne \varnothing$, and $DL\vert_{v_n} \to \infty$ whenever $\operatorname{dom}L\cap T_xM \ni v_n \to v \in \partial\operatorname{dom}L$;
  • (IV) $\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}L\cap T_xM = \operatorname{int}(\operatorname{dom}L\cap T_xM)$ for every $x \in M$;
  • (V) $0 < L < \infty$ on the zero section.

Let $\mathcal{A} \subseteq M \times M$ be such that:

  • (VI) every $(x_0,x_1) \in \mathcal{A}$ is joined by a minimizing extremal;
  • (VII) for every compact $K \subseteq \mathcal{A}$ there are a compact $V \subseteq \operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}L$ and $T > 0$ such that every minimizing extremal $\gamma : [0,\ell] \to M$ with $(\gamma(0),\gamma(\ell)) \in K$ has $\ell \le T$ and $\dot\gamma(t) \in V$ for all $t$.

Departures from the Tonelli setting: $L$ may take the value $+\infty$, and $L$ need not be fiberwise superlinear.

Theorem (A. & Manini '26). Let $\mathfrak{m}$ be a measure on $M$ with a smooth positive density, and let $\mu_0 = f_0\,\mathfrak{m}$, $\mu_1 = f_1\,\mathfrak{m}$ be compactly supported, absolutely continuous Borel probability measures with $\mu_0 \perp \mu_1$ and $\operatorname{supp}\mu_0\times\operatorname{supp}\mu_1 \subseteq \mathcal{A}$.

Then there exist a family $\Gamma$ of minimizing extremals, a probability measure $\nu$ on $\Gamma$, Borel probability measures $(\mathfrak{m}_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}$ on $M$, and a Borel set $\mathcal{T}$ of full $(\mu_0+\mu_1)$-measure such that:

  1. Disintegration: for every $\mathfrak{m}$-measurable $A \subseteq \mathcal{T}$, $\ \gamma \mapsto \mathfrak{m}_\gamma(A)$ is $\nu$-measurable and $\ \displaystyle \mathfrak{m}(A) = \int_\Gamma \mathfrak{m}_\gamma(A)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\gamma)$.
  2. Needles: for $\nu$-a.e. $\gamma$, $\ \mathfrak{m}_\gamma = \gamma_\sharp(m_\gamma)$, where $m_\gamma$ is a measure on the interval $(0,\ell_\gamma)$ with a smooth, positive density $e^{-\psi_\gamma}$.
  3. Mass balance: for $\nu$-a.e. $\gamma$, $\ \displaystyle \int f_0\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_\gamma = \int f_1\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_\gamma$ $\ $ and $\ \displaystyle \int_t^{\ell_\gamma} f_0\circ\gamma\,\mathrm{d}m_\gamma \le \int_t^{\ell_\gamma} f_1\circ\gamma\,\mathrm{d}m_\gamma$ for all $t \in (0,\ell_\gamma)$.
  4. Hamilton–Jacobi: for $\nu$-a.e. $\gamma$ and every $t \in (0,\ell_\gamma)$ there is a smooth solution $u_{\gamma,t}$ of $H(\mathrm{d}u_{\gamma,t}) = 0$ on a neighborhood $U_{\gamma,t} \ni \gamma(t)$ such that, on $\gamma^{-1}(U_{\gamma,t})$, $$\dot\gamma = (\mathcal{L}\,\mathrm{d}u_{\gamma,t})\circ\gamma \qquad\text{and}\qquad -\dot\psi_\gamma = \big(\operatorname{div}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathcal{L}\,\mathrm{d}u_{\gamma,t})\big)\circ\gamma.$$ Here $H$ is the Hamiltonian and $\mathcal{L}$ the Legendre transform.

Proof sketch

Lagrangian $L : TM \to \mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$ satisfying (I)–(V); $\ $ set $\mathcal{A} \subseteq M\times M$ satisfying (VI)–(VII); $\ $reference measure $\mathfrak{m}$; $\ $probability measures $\mu_0 = f_0\,\mathfrak{m} \perp \mu_1 = f_1\,\mathfrak{m}$, $\ \operatorname{supp}\mu_0\times\operatorname{supp}\mu_1 \subseteq \mathcal{A}$.

  1. The Kantorovich potential $u$ for $(\mu_0, \mu_1)$ induces a decomposition of $M$ into disjoint minimizing extremals (transport rays), which solve $\dot\gamma = \mathcal{L}\,\mathrm{d}u$ on the set where $u$ is differentiable. $\mathcal{L}:=$ Legendre transform.
  2. Near each point on a ray, modify $L$ away from the transport direction so that it becomes finite, superlinear and strictly convex, and rays passing nearby are also transport rays of a Kantorovich potential for the regularized Lagrangian.
  3. Apply needle decomposition result for Tonelli Lagrangians (A. '26) to the regularized Lagrangian. Since it agrees with $L$ along the rays, the local needles are pieces of the global rays, and the Hamilton–Jacobi equations coincide.
  4. The densities of the local needle match up to multiplicative constants, so they can be glued together to form a global needle decomposition.
  5. Mass balance is proved as usual.
